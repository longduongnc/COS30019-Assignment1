# Research log (Weeks 3–4)

## Search approach
- Followed citations forwards and backwards from Hagendorff (2020).
- Searched for empirical studies of practitioners (interviews, surveys, experiments) and for Australian evidence on uptake of the AI Ethics Principles.
- Checked bibliographic details of every academic source against Crossref.

## Evidence by key issue

| Key issue | Source | Type of evidence | What it shows |
|-----------|--------|------------------|---------------|
| 1. Individual decisions | McNamara et al. (2018) | Controlled experiment, n = 168 | Seeing the ACM code made no significant difference to decisions |
| 1 | Pant et al. (2024) | Survey, n = 100 AI practitioners | Most are familiar with AI ethics, mainly through workplace policies |
| 1 | Sanderson et al. (2023) | 21 interviews at CSIRO, against Australia's 8 principles | Principles interpreted inconsistently; trade-offs left to developers |
| 2. Organisational pressure | Ali et al. (2023) | Qualitative study of ethics workers in tech companies | Policies, practices and outcomes are decoupled; three barriers |
| 2 | Rakova et al. (2021) | 26 interviews, 19 organisations | Responsible AI work is mostly reactive; incentives misaligned |
| 2 | Fifth Quadrant (2024) | Survey, n = 413 Australian executives | 78% say they align with the Principles, 29% have implemented practices |
| 2 | Piper (2019); Schiffer & Newton (2023) | News reports | Ethics structures removed at Google and Microsoft |
| 3. Principles to practice | Mittelstadt (2019); Morley et al. (2020) | Conceptual analysis; review of tools | Focus on "what" not "how"; methods immature |
| 3 | Sadek et al. (2025) | Scoping review | Five reasons guidelines fail to affect practice |
| 3 | DISR (2025a) | Government guidance | Six action-based practices replace value statements |
| 4. Voluntary vs binding | Munn (2023); Hagendorff (2020) | Critical analysis | Principles are "toothless" without consequences |
| 4 | DISR (2024, 2025b); Regulation 2024/1689 | Policy and law | Australia stayed voluntary; the EU legislated |

## Conflicting views recorded
- McNamara et al. show no effect from reading a code, but Pant et al. show that workplace policies do build awareness.
- Munn calls principles "useless", while Jobin et al. show they have produced international agreement on values that regulators can build on.
- The Responsible AI Index shows a large say-do gap overall, but also that organisations with leadership-driven AI strategy implement far more practices.
